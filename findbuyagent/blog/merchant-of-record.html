merchant-of-record

· 2 min read · FindBuyAgent

In this essay

A quiet fear in retail is that the agent platform becomes the only store: one chat window, every brand behind it, the customer relationship captured by whoever owns the model. The fear is reasonable. It is also a choice, not a law of software. A Find & Buy Agent can buy from the merchant who actually sells the thing.

Merchant of record is a legal job, not a UI job

The merchant of record is who the customer is buying from: who charges, who issues the receipt, who owns the return, who sits in front of the acquirer. If an agent platform inserts itself as that merchant for every purchase, it has built a marketplace and should be regulated and branded as one. If it does not, the retailer’s existing checkout remains the checkout. The agent is a buyer’s representative, the way a procurement clerk is, not a new storefront with a take rate hidden in the prompt.

Open storefront protocols — the ones retailers have pushed so they are not scraped into someone else’s mall — exist to keep that job where it is. The agent asks a structured catalog for price, tax, and delivery. It settles with a credential the retailer’s acquirer already understands. Visa’s side of that settlement is a token and a mandate. The retailer’s side is still the order.

Disputes get simpler when both sides wrote down the offer

When software buys, “I didn’t mean to” splits into cases, and they should not all be called fraud.

  • Inside the mandate. The offer matched the ceiling, the item, and the deadline the person approved. The merchant delivered that offer. Unhappiness is a return, not a chargeback. The pilot logic of an approved invoice lives here.
  • Outside the mandate. The person approved noon and no shellfish. The agent accepted four o’clock and shellfish. That failure belongs to the agent’s operator, not to a merchant who told the truth in the feed.
  • A dishonest feed. The merchant’s structured offer said refundable, or said wool, and the thing was neither. The agent did what it was told by the seller. The seller owns that.

None of those cases require the agent platform to become the merchant. They require the offer and the mandate to be specific enough to compare. A chat transcript is a bad exhibit. A signed instruction and a structured offer are a good one.

What this means on the phone screen

Show the seller’s name next to the price. Do not say “we bought it” if a retailer sold it. Do not hide whose return policy applies. The Find & Buy Agent can be relentless about the price and still be modest about the role. It found. The person approved. The merchant sold. The network, if it is a card, moved a token. Confusing those four jobs is how a useful agent becomes a marketplace nobody agreed to join.
