How agents pay

The open standards behind agent payments, and where FindBuyAgent fits. FindBuyAgent uses x402 today.

x402

The client requests a resource. The server answers 402 with an accepts array: scheme exact, network, asset, amount, and payTo. The wallet signs an EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization (EIP-712). The client retries with the payment header. A facilitator verifies and settles. The server then returns 200 and the settlement response. Coinbase’s agent-wallet notes say a wallet can enforce a per-payment cap, a rolling cap, and a payee allowlist. FindBuyAgent enforces those rules in the app before it asks your wallet to sign.

Who signs what

Your wallet signs the exact USDC authorization. FindBuyAgent never sees the key. The facilitator broadcasts the settlement. The merchant does not get a standing allowance from this signature.

AP2

AP2 uses a Checkout Mandate and a Payment Mandate. Each can be open (constraints for autonomous buys) or closed (an exact cart). They are signed verifiable credentials from a trusted surface, and the flow ends with a signed Payment Receipt. The protocol is rail-agnostic. x402 can be the payment method inside it. FindBuyAgent mandate ≈ AP2 open mandate; the approval sheet ≈ closing it.

Who signs what

A trusted surface signs the mandates as verifiable credentials. FindBuyAgent’s in-browser signature records your rules. It is not an AP2 credential, and it does not move funds.

Checkout Mandate (open): constraints, not one cart
Payment Mandate: who pays, on which rail
Payment Receipt: signed after settlement
x402 can be the rail inside the payment mandate

ACP

ACP is a merchant checkout. The agent calls POST /checkout_sessions, can update the session, then POST /complete or /cancel. Order webhooks tell the agent what happened. Payment is a delegated token with a max amount and an expiry, such as a Stripe Shared Payment Token. The merchant stays merchant of record. In March 2026 OpenAI said it would deprioritize native Instant Checkout in ChatGPT in favour of merchant checkouts and apps.

Who signs what

The person confirms in the chat. The payment token is issued by the PSP, capped and expiring. The merchant charges it. FindBuyAgent does not issue ACP tokens.

POST /checkout_sessions
POST /checkout_sessions/{id}
POST /checkout_sessions/{id}/complete
POST /checkout_sessions/{id}/cancel
Delegated payment token: max amount + expiry

Visa TAP

Registered agents sign HTTP requests with Web Bot Auth (RFC 9421). The signature can carry a browse or pay tag and a nonce. Payment uses agentic network tokens rather than a raw card number.

Who signs what

The agent signs the HTTP message, not an EIP-3009 transfer. The network token pays. FindBuyAgent does not register a Visa agent and does not hold a card token.

Signature-Input: web-bot-auth; alg="ed25519"
tag: browse | pay
nonce: one-time
payment: agentic network token

Sources: Cloudflare on agentic commerce · Visa Intelligent Commerce

Mastercard Agent Pay

Mastercard Agent Pay is in the same family: a registered agent presents a signed request, and an agentic network token pays inside the limits the person set. FindBuyAgent does not settle those tokens.

Who signs what

The network, not this site, holds the card credential. The person sets the instruction. FindBuyAgent’s path is the opposite shape: you hold the key and sign USDC yourself.

Agent Pay: registered agent + network token
FindBuyAgent: your wallet + x402 USDC
These are different rails.

How they fit

The protocols stack. Identity says who is asking. Authorization says what they may do. Checkout builds the order. Settlement moves the money. FindBuyAgent sits in authorization and, when you sign, in x402 settlement.

  1. IdentityTAP, Web Bot Auth, KYA
  2. AuthorizationAP2 mandates, FindBuyAgent rules
  3. CheckoutACP, UCP, merchant site
  4. Settlementx402 on-chain, card network tokens
